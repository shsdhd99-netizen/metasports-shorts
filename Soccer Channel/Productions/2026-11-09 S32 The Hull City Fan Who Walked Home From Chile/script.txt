The Hull City fan who walked home from Chile. Back in {1998|nineteen ninety-eight}, a former paratrooper from Hull, Karl Bushby, stood at the southern tip of South America with one plan: walk home. No cars, no planes, no boats. And no going back to Britain until he'd walked there. He thought it would take twelve years. He crossed jungles and deserts. He was jailed in Panama for entering without a visa. He crossed the frozen Bering Strait on foot, from Alaska into Russia. Later, Russia banned him from the country. So he walked from Los Angeles to the Russian embassy in Washington to protest, and the ban was lifted. This October, he swam the English Channel. And in November, he walked into Hull. His mother, his son and his granddaughter were waiting. So was his football club. Hull City posted his video, in a Hull shirt, from across the river: tomorrow I'm crossing that bridge. It took him twenty-eight years. But here's the wild part. The month he set off, Hull City were bottom of the Football League's lowest division, heading out of the league and into bankruptcy. He walked home to a Premier League club.
