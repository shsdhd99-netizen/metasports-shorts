The England player who kept Maradona's Hand of God shirt. Back in 1986, England played Argentina in a World Cup quarter-final, and Argentina had Diego Maradona. Six minutes into the second half, an England midfielder called Steve Hodge tried a back-pass to his goalkeeper, Peter Shilton. But Maradona jumped and punched the ball into the net with his hand. The referee gave the goal. Maradona later said it was scored a little with his head, and a little with the hand of God. Four minutes later, he dribbled through the England team and scored again. That one was voted the goal of the century. Argentina won {2-1|two-one} and went on to win the World Cup. But it got even crazier. Under the stadium after the game, Hodge saw Maradona and tugged on his own shirt, as if to say, any chance of swapping? Maradona came straight over, made a little prayer sign, and they swapped. So the England player who set up the hand of God went home with the shirt that scored it. From 2002, it was on show at England's National Football Museum. But here's the wild part. In 2022, Hodge sold it at auction for {£7.1 million|seven point one million pounds}, the most ever paid for a match-worn sports shirt at the time.
