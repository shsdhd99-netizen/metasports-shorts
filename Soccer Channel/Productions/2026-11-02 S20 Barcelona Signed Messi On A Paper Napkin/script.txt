Barcelona signed Messi on a paper napkin. Back in {2000|two thousand}, Lionel Messi was a small kid in Rosario, Argentina. He needed growth hormone treatment. Reportedly, about a thousand dollars a month. Newell's Old Boys and River Plate both wanted him. Neither could afford it. So Messi went to Barcelona for a trial. But the club was slow. According to one of the men who brought him there, Barcelona said it would be ten or twelve years before they saw the benefit. His father was reportedly tired of waiting. So on the fourteenth of December, Barcelona's Carles Rexach sat down to lunch at a tennis club with two of the men behind the move. And he wrote the deal on a paper napkin. He agreed, "regardless of any dissenting opinions", to sign the player Lionel Messi. Messi was thirteen. A formal contract followed. He became Barcelona's all-time top scorer, with six hundred and seventy-two goals. Then, in {2021|twenty twenty-one}, Barcelona said they had agreed a new contract with him. But financial and structural obstacles stopped it. Messi cried at his goodbye. He said he was not ready to leave. Three years later, that napkin sold at auction for seven hundred and sixty-two thousand pounds. Almost a million dollars. Money problems pushed Messi out of Barcelona. But the napkin that brought him in was worth a fortune.
