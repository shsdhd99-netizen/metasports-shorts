Barcelona signed Lionel Messi on a paper napkin. Back in {2000|two thousand}, Messi was a tiny kid from Rosario, in Argentina. He needed growth hormone treatment. About a thousand dollars a month. Newell's Old Boys and River Plate both wanted him. Neither could afford it. So he went to Barcelona for a trial. But the club was slow. Some of them said it would be ten or twelve years before they saw the benefit. His father was tired of waiting. So on the fourteenth of December, Barcelona's sporting director, Carles Rexach, met two of Messi's agents for lunch at a tennis club. And he wrote the deal on a paper napkin. He agreed, "regardless of any dissenting opinions", to sign the player Lionel Messi. Messi was thirteen. He became Barcelona's all-time top scorer. Then, in {2021|twenty twenty-one}, Barcelona said they had agreed a new contract with him. But under LaLiga's rules, they couldn't afford to register it. Messi left in tears. Three years later, that napkin sold at auction for seven hundred and sixty-two thousand pounds. Almost a million dollars. Barcelona couldn't afford to keep Messi. But the napkin that signed him was worth a fortune.
