The England subs brought on only for penalties. Back in {2021|twenty twenty-one}, England played Italy in the final of the Euros, at Wembley. After extra time, it was still level. Then, in the very last minute, Gareth Southgate made two changes. He took off Kyle Walker and Jordan Henderson, and brought on Marcus Rashford and Jadon Sancho. Not to win the game. Just to take penalties. On the BBC, commentator Guy Mowbray had even feared Italy would keep the ball, to stop England making those changes. Years earlier, in {2014|twenty fourteen}, Louis van Gaal had done something similar, bringing on a goalkeeper for the shootout, and it worked. Italy kicked first. Berardi scored. Kane scored. Pickford saved Belotti's, and Harry Maguire put England ahead. But Bonucci levelled it. Then Rashford walked up, and hit the post. Italy scored again. Then came Sancho. Donnarumma saved it. England were nearly out, but Pickford saved Jorginho's. Then Bukayo Saka stepped up, and Donnarumma saved that one too. Italy won {3-2|three-two}. Afterwards, Southgate called the substitutions a gamble, and said the order of takers had been set before the match. Van Gaal's substitute saved two penalties. Southgate's two substitutes missed theirs.
