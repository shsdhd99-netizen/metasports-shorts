Goalkeepers who hid a cheat sheet in their sock. Back in 2006, Germany hosted the World Cup, and in the quarterfinal they faced Argentina in Berlin. After 120 minutes it was still {1-1|one-one}, so the game went to penalties. But Germany's keeper, Jens Lehmann, had a secret. His goalkeeping coach, Andreas Köpke, had studied where Argentina's players liked to shoot, and the tips ended up on a tiny page from the team hotel's notepad. Riquelme, left high. Heinze, left low. Even Messi was on it. Lehmann tucked the paper into his sock. And before Argentina's kicks, he kept pulling it out to read it, right in front of the whole stadium. But there was one big problem. Only two of the players on his list actually took a penalty. He saved Roberto Ayala's, and he nearly saved Maxi Rodríguez's. Then came the kick that would decide everything. Esteban Cambiasso walked up, and his name was nowhere on that paper. So Lehmann pulled it out anyway. He read it, nodded, and put it away, like he knew exactly what was coming. Cambiasso shot, and Lehmann saved it. Germany won {4-2|four-two}. But here's the wild part. After the game, Lehmann found the note lying on the dressing room floor, next to his socks. Months later, the boss of a German energy company bought it for one million euros, at a charity auction for children. One million euros, for a page from a hotel notepad.
