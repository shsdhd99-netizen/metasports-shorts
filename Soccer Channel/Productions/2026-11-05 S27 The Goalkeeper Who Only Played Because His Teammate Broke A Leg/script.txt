The goalkeeper who only played because his teammate broke a leg. Back in {1990|nineteen ninety}, Argentina went to the World Cup with Nery Pumpido in goal, a world champion. Then, in their second game, against the Soviet Union, Pumpido broke his leg. Eleven minutes in. His understudy, Sergio Goycochea, took over for the rest of the tournament. In the quarter-final against Yugoslavia, it finished goalless, so it went to penalties. Diego Maradona missed. Pedro Troglio missed. Yugoslavia were on the brink. Goycochea told Maradona, Relax, I'm going to save a couple. He later admitted that was more wishful thinking than confidence. Then he dived low to his right, and saved Brnovic. And with Yugoslavia's last kick, he flung himself to his left, and saved Hadzibegic. Argentina were through. In the semi-final, against Italy, in Naples, it was level again, and penalties again. Goycochea saved Donadoni. Then he saved Serena. That made four saves, a record for a single World Cup. But here's the wild part. In the final, West Germany won one-nil, from a penalty. The keeper who only played because of a broken leg had saved four to get Argentina there, and lost it to one.
