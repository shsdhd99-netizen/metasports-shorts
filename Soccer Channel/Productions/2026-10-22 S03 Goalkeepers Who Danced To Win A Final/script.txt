Goalkeepers who danced on the line to win a final. Back in 2005, Liverpool played AC Milan in the Champions League final in Istanbul. Milan had Maldini, Pirlo, Kaká and Shevchenko, and Paolo Maldini scored inside the first minute. By half-time, Milan were winning {3-0|three-nil}. But then came six minutes nobody saw coming. Steven Gerrard scored with a header. {Vladimír Šmicer|Vladimir Shmitser} scored from distance. Then Xabi Alonso's penalty was saved, and he scored the rebound. {3-3|Three-three}. Then, three minutes from the end of extra time, Shevchenko had two shots from right in front of goal. And Jerzy Dudek saved both. In 2011, a UEFA poll voted it the greatest moment in Champions League history. So it went to penalties. Dudek had studied about a hundred of Milan's penalties, and his goalkeeping coach was ready to signal left or right. But then Jamie Carragher jumped on his back with a different idea. Do what Bruce Grobbelaar did. You see, in 1984, Liverpool won the same trophy on penalties, against Roma, in Rome, and Grobbelaar wobbled his legs on the line. He said later, I'm in Rome, the national dish is spaghetti, so I'll do spaghetti legs. Two Roma players missed. So Dudek started dancing on his line. Serginho blasted Milan's first penalty over the bar. Dudek saved Andrea Pirlo's. And when Shevchenko had to score to keep Milan alive, Dudek saved that one too. Liverpool were champions of Europe. But here's the wild part. That summer, Liverpool signed a new goalkeeper, Pepe Reina. And in the two years that followed, the hero of Istanbul played just twelve games.
