Ronaldo moved two Coca-Cola bottles, and billions disappeared.

Back in June {2021|twenty twenty-one}, Cristiano Ronaldo walked into a press conference in Budapest.

It was the day before Portugal's first game at Euro {2020|twenty twenty}.

On the table in front of him were two bottles of Coca-Cola.

Coca-Cola was an official sponsor of the tournament.

Ronaldo looked at the bottles. He grabbed them. And he moved them out of the camera's view.

Then he held up a bottle of water, and said: "Agua."

The clip went everywhere.

The official reply: everyone is entitled to their drink preferences, and players are offered water, Coke, or Coke Zero.

But headlines said Ronaldo had wiped {$4 billion|four billion dollars} off Coca-Cola.

The numbers looked real. Coca-Cola's market value did go from about {$242 billion|two hundred forty-two billion} to {$238 billion|two hundred thirty-eight billion}.

But here's the part the headlines left out.

Snopes found the stock was already falling before he even sat down. And after his press conference, it briefly went up.

The next day, Ronaldo played Hungary.

In front of a packed stadium, Portugal won {3-0|three-nil}. Ronaldo scored twice, in the last minutes, and became the Euros' all-time top scorer, with eleven goals.

One man moved two bottles, and the internet gave him the credit for billions.

And there's no sign he cost Coca-Cola a cent.
