The manager who told his team to stop scoring against Manchester United. Back in {2004|two thousand and four}, a {17|seventeen}-year-old Cesc Fabregas, who would later captain Arsenal, was in the Old Trafford tunnel, when a slice of pizza hit Sir Alex Ferguson. {13|Thirteen} years later, he admitted on TV that he threw it. Today, Fabregas manages, and partly owns, Como, a club that went bankrupt twice, and was playing in Italy's fifth division the year United last won the Champions League. Last week, in their first ever Champions League season, Como hosted Manchester United. Nico Paz scored after just {118|a hundred and eighteen} seconds. Then he set one up. Then he scored again. Then it was four-nil. And that's when Fabregas did something managers never do. He told his players to stop running. I told the players to stop running and stay in the middle, he said. At four-nil, we could manage things a little. In stoppage time, United pulled one back. Their manager apologised to the fans, and his own captain reportedly questioned his tactics. United were now seven games without a win, their worst run since {2015|twenty fifteen}. But the strangest apology came from the man who won. I regret the last ten minutes, Fabregas said. It was my fault. {22|Twenty-two} years after the pizza, his only regret against Manchester United was letting them score.
