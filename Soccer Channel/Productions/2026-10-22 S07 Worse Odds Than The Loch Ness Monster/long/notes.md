## Fact check (Oct 23, 2026)

> **READ FIRST: 2 WRONG lines and 3 to reword. This voice needs a re-record (the payoff is affected).**
> 1. **WRONG (line 6): "William Hill gave better odds on finding Elvis alive, and on finding the Loch Ness monster."** William Hill's own price on both was the **same** 5,000-1, not better:
>    - Sports Illustrated (Mar 2016) lists Elvis among the "5,000 to 1 odds with William Hill";
>    - Slate (May 2016): William Hill "offers the same odds on whether the Loch Ness monster will turn up";
>    - Oxera (May 2016) says the same.
>
>    Shorter odds came only from other bookmakers: AP gives Elvis at 2,000-1 with no bookmaker named, and Paddy Power reportedly rated Elvis more likely. For Nessie, the only sources are an affiliate site (BettingOdds.com, Paddy Power 500/1) and Ranieri's second-hand memory (FourFourTwo 2026, "I know this because somebody told me").
> 2. **NEEDS REWORDING (title, line 1): "worse odds than the Loch Ness monster".** The best-sourced comparison (William Hill) is *equal* odds. "Worse" rests only on Ranieri's second-hand quote and BettingOdds.com. The owner chooses:
>    - **A (safest):** retitle "The team with the same odds as the Loch Ness monster". Line 6 becomes "William Hill gave the same odds on finding Elvis alive, or the Loch Ness monster."
>    - **B (keeps the hook):** keep the title. Line 6 becomes "Bookmakers reportedly rated finding Elvis alive, or the Loch Ness monster, as more likely."
> 3. **WRONG (line 19): "And in 130 years, Ladbrokes had never paid out a bet at odds that long."** Ladbrokes' 130-year claim was about the *size* of the payout: "the biggest anti-post payout in our 130-year history". The "never paid odds that long" claims came from Sky Bet ("first time they have ever paid out a single outright bet at 5,000/1") and William Hill. Fix: "Ladbrokes called it the biggest payout of its kind in its 130-year history."
> 4. **NEEDS REWORDING (line 20, the payoff): "Bookmakers called it the longest-odds win ever bet on, in any sport."** No bookmaker said this. What they did say:
>    - bookmakers called it the "biggest shock in the history of betting" (Euronews);
>    - Ladbrokes: "This is the biggest sporting upset of all time";
>    - Sky Sports: the "greatest sporting upset of the modern era, according to the bookmakers".
>
>    Fix: "And bookmakers called it the biggest shock in the history of betting."
> 5. **NEEDS REWORDING, minor (line 8): "Then Leicester hired Claudio Ranieri, a manager who had just been sacked by Greece."**
>    - **"Then":** Ranieri was hired on 13 July 2015, and the 5,000-1 price was still on offer in August (Ladbrokes, Paddy Power), so "Then" gets the order wrong.
>    - **"just":** Greece sacked him on 15 Nov 2014, eight months before.
>    - **Cut-only fix:** remove "Then" (22.536-22.664 s) and "just" (24.858-25.021 s).
>    - **Re-voice fix:** "Leicester had just hired Claudio Ranieri, a manager who had been sacked by Greece after losing to the Faroe Islands." That adds a sourced, absurd detail.
>
> Lines 6, 19 and 20 need new words (the last line can't be fixed by cutting), so re-record the whole script in one take (about 1,600 credits). A corrected script is at the bottom.
>
> **Sources disagree on line 17:** "47 bets". City AM, CBS and ESPN say 47, with 24-25 cashed out. JOE and PokerNews (quoting Ladbrokes a day later) say 57, with 36 cashed out. Keeping "47" with "around half" is consistent with the 47 sources. If you want it true under both, say "about fifty bets" and "more than half".
