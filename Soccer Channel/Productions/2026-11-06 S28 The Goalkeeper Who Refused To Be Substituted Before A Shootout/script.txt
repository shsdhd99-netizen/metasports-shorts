The goalkeeper who refused to be substituted before a shootout. Back in {2019|twenty nineteen}, Chelsea played Manchester City in the League Cup final at Wembley. Still goalless, deep into extra time. Chelsea's goalkeeper, Kepa Arrizabalaga, was struggling with cramp. So his manager, Maurizio Sarri, got a substitute ready for the penalties: Willy Caballero. A shootout specialist. Three years earlier, Caballero had saved three penalties to win this same trophy for Manchester City. The board went up with Kepa's number. And Kepa waved it away. The referee, Jon Moss, asked him to leave. Kepa still refused. Sarri threw down a water bottle and nearly marched into the tunnel. And Kepa stayed on. Then came the shootout. Jorginho missed. Chelsea's keeper let Sergio Aguero's weak penalty go under him. Then he saved Leroy Sane's. But it wasn't enough. Raheem Sterling scored the last kick, and City won four-three. Kepa was fined a week's wages. Sarri dropped him for the next game. Later he said, I am not proud about what happened. But here's the strange part. He wouldn't make way for the man who'd saved three penalties to win this trophy. He saved one, and lost it to that man's old club.
