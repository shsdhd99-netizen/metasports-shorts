Barcelona paid a fortune for the player who knocked them out. Back in January {2018|twenty eighteen}, Barcelona signed Philippe Coutinho from Liverpool, for a reported {160 million|one hundred and sixty million} euros, one of the most expensive transfers ever. But it never clicked. Meanwhile, Liverpool added Virgil van Dijk and Alisson, and won the Champions League in {2019|twenty nineteen}. That summer, Barcelona loaned Coutinho to Bayern Munich, who paid just {8.5 million|eight and a half million} euros to borrow him. Then, in August {2020|twenty twenty}, Barcelona met Bayern in the Champions League quarter-final, in Lisbon. Bayern destroyed Messi's Barcelona. It was the first time Barcelona had conceded eight goals in a match since {1946|nineteen forty-six}. And the man who finished them off? Philippe Coutinho. He came off the bench, set up a goal, then scored two more, against the club that still owned him. He barely celebrated. Bayern went on to win the whole competition, and Coutinho played in the final. Bayern had the option to buy him for {120 million|one hundred and twenty million} euros. They didn't take it. So he went back to Barcelona, who sold him to Aston Villa two years later, for {20 million|twenty million} euros. They paid a reported {160 million|one hundred and sixty million} for the man who knocked them out, and got {20 million|twenty million} back.
