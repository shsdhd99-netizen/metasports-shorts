The goalkeeper who saved a shot with a scorpion kick at Wembley. And it was the first time he'd ever tried it in a real game. Back in 1995, Colombia played England in a friendly, and their keeper, René Higuita, had a move nobody had seen. He got the idea filming a soda advert, when a kid kicked a ball at him and he flicked it away with both heels behind his back. So he practised it. In training. In warm-ups. For years. But never in a match. Then, at Wembley, England's Jamie Redknapp, on his debut, miscued a cross. It floated straight at Higuita. Any keeper in the world just catches that. Higuita let it drop over his head, dived forward, and kicked it clear with his heels, like a scorpion's tail. The commentator thought the whistle must have gone. England's coaches were laughing on the bench. The game finished nil-nil, so the only thing anyone remembers is the save. And it should never have happened. The linesman's flag was already up for offside. The referee just hadn't seen it.
