# 30 — Why a heave miss is free

Not voiced. Stopped on Oct 5, 2026, after the six downloads. Each file moves. Five of them are makes, and the sixth is a fourth-quarter ending with no heave in the sampled frames. A make cannot sit under "a miss does not count." No post-rule miss is on disk, so there is no voice, no cut, and no assemble.

Lane: rule
Concept: A miss from 36 feet in the last three seconds of the first three quarters does not hit the shooter's percentage. A make still does. A fourth-quarter miss still does.
Added value: The free miss stops at the fourth quarter, and a make is still one for one.

Copy-check on Oct 5, 2026, from the repo: `NBA heave rule` was FRESH. Top storyteller was USA TODAY NEWS at 101 views. A second query, `heave miss is free`, also printed FRESH, but those hits were songs about heaven, not this rule. The first check is the one that counts.

Do not say the fourth quarter is free. NBA.com's Nov. 6, 2025 feature says "any of the four quarters." The Associated Press, ESPN, and the wording Sporting News quotes as the league's own line all say the first three quarters. The script uses the first three quarters.

Do not say half-court is the line. The line is 36 feet. Half-court is farther. Do not use last season's Basketball-Reference "backcourt shot" totals (Jokic 22, 41.7 percent to 44.3 percent). That page says those were the old tracking set, not the new 36-foot set.
