# Script template and worked examples

## Template (255-285 words, about 65 s)
1. **[0-2 s] TITLE as a sentence.** "<Category of people> who <did the absurd thing>." This is the spoken title and the YouTube title.
2. **[2-10 s] SETUP.** "Back in <year>, <famous name / team> <the stakes in one sentence>." Add one more sentence of context: the match, the score, why it mattered.
3. **[10-17 s] TURN 1.** "But <name> had a secret / there was one big problem / the phone call never came." Name the small human or absurd detail here.
4. **[17-45 s] ESCALATION, 2-4 beats.**
   - Concrete details with names and numbers. Each sentence is one picture.
   - Use "But it got even crazier", "There was just one problem", "Then came the moment that decided everything".
5. **[45-55 s] CLIMAX.** The moment itself, in short sentences: "Cambiasso shot, and Lehmann saved it. Germany won four-two."
6. **[55-62 s] PAYOFF.** "But here's the wild part." Then the irony or the hard number: "One million euros, for a page from a hotel notepad."
7. **[optional, 62-68 s] TEASER.** "And my next video is about <next Short>. Follow if you want the algorithm to show it to you."

## Checklist before voicing
- [ ] Line 1 = the title; a famous name within the first 10 s.
- [ ] A turn by about 15 s, and 2+ escalations.
- [ ] Every number and date checked against the source's own timeline, and qualifiers kept.
- [ ] The payoff is a number or irony; no question ending.
- [ ] Scores spelled for the voice: `{6-1|six-one}`.
- [ ] Every sentence can be shown with a real picture (or a labelled recreation).
- [ ] 255-285 words.

## Worked example 1: S01 (66 s, QA PASS)
> Goalkeepers who hid a cheat sheet in their sock. Back in 2006, Germany hosted the World Cup, and in the quarterfinal they faced Argentina in Berlin. After 120 minutes it was still {1-1|one-one}, so the game went to penalties. But Germany's keeper, Jens Lehmann, had a secret. His goalkeeping coach, Andreas Köpke, had studied where Argentina's players liked to shoot, and the tips ended up on a tiny page from the team hotel's notepad. Riquelme, left high. Heinze, left low. Even Messi was on it. Lehmann tucked the paper into his sock. And before Argentina's kicks, he kept pulling it out to read it, right in front of the whole stadium. But there was one big problem. Only two of the players on his list actually took a penalty. He saved Roberto Ayala's, and he nearly saved Maxi Rodríguez's. Then came the kick that would decide everything. Esteban Cambiasso walked up, and his name was nowhere on that paper. So Lehmann pulled it out anyway. He read it, nodded, and put it away, like he knew exactly what was coming. Cambiasso shot, and Lehmann saved it. Germany won {4-2|four-two}. But here's the wild part. After the game, Lehmann found the note lying on the dressing room floor, next to his socks. Months later, the boss of a German energy company bought it for one million euros, at a charity auction for children. One million euros, for a page from a hotel notepad. And my next video is about the fire at Mario Balotelli's house, the day before the Manchester derby. Follow if you want the algorithm to show it to you.

## Worked example 2: S02 (64 s)
> Players who got in trouble the day before a derby. Back in October 2011, Manchester City were about to play Manchester United at Old Trafford, and up front for City was Mario Balotelli. But just before 1 a.m. on the day before the derby, two fire crews were called to his house. Balotelli had four friends over, and they were letting off fireworks out of the bathroom window. The towels caught fire, and the bathroom went up in flames. Firefighters had to go in with breathing apparatus. And reports say Balotelli even ran back inside to grab a suitcase and some cash, so the police had to make sure he stayed out. By the morning, it was all over the news. But Balotelli checked into a hotel, and at 10 a.m. he turned up for training like nothing had happened. And Roberto Mancini still put him in the team. After 22 minutes, Balotelli scored. Then he lifted his shirt over his head to show a message underneath. Why always me? The referee booked him for it. But it got even crazier. In the second half, Balotelli scored again, and City just kept going. Three, four, five, six. Manchester City won {6-1|six-one}, United's worst home defeat since 1955, and City went five points clear at the top. But here's the wild part. That shirt had nothing to do with the fire. Balotelli asked the kit man to print it on the Monday before the derby, days before any fire. And the day after the derby, the man whose bathroom had caught fire was telling Manchester to follow the firework code.
