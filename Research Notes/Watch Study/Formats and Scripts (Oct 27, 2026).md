# Competitor formats and scripts: what hits do that misses don't (Gemini studies, Oct 27, 2026)

**The owner asked (Oct 27):** "review more shorts, and competitor channel editing formats, and scripts".

**Sample:** about 180 Shorts, watched free on Gemini.
- **Narrated channels:** each channel's top 10 and bottom 10 of its newest 60 Shorts (the 5 newest skipped).
  - NBA: Swishyyy, Hoopiverse, CoachClank, Ahmed's Basketball and Rebound Rewind (100 Shorts).
  - Soccer: Ahmed's Goal in two windows (40).
- **Format survey:** the top 5 of 8 channels with other formats (40):
  - rankings: roycebball;
  - one clip plus one caption: ChazNBA, breakawayclips;
  - clip edits with story text: docbooo;
  - "last one is crazy" compilations: SparkBalls;
  - elimination games: Ftblpranz, footballgoat7;
  - "getting cooked" compilations: TAT_futbol.
- **Scripts:** the word-for-word narration of 86 NBA and 36 soccer Shorts (Gemini transcripts).

**Data in this folder:**
- the study CSVs and their summaries;
- `Script study (2026-10-27).csv` and its summary;
- `Script Library (2026-10-27).md`.

**Tier:** all [S]. Gemini coded everything, n is about 10 per group per channel, and it's correlation. Gemini's labels moved a few points between two runs of the script study, so read small gaps as noise.

- **The most reliable loser on every narrated channel is the current news take or question:** "Is X about to...?", "Are they doomed?", "How much does the injury hurt?".
- **Also losing:** sentimental fan moments, money and fines, and rule trivia.
- **Hits:** something anyone gets in a second (size, age, a spectacle, kids, a famous blunder), or a list with a clever frame.

## 2. Lists with a clever frame win on the explainer channels
- **Rebound Rewind:** 7 of its 10 hits are lists ("at every age", "at every height", "How many NBA players are left from the 2000s?"); none of its 10 misses are.
- **CoachClank, Ahmed's Basketball and Rebound Rewind:** a countdown or ranking device appears in 14% of their hits and 0% of their misses.
- **The format survey's biggest Shorts are rankings and elimination games:**
  - roycebball: "Ranking 1 in a Trillion Basketball Moments" 39M;
  - Ftblpranz: 29M;
  - footballgoat7: 15M.

## 3. Editing
- **Cards:** hits have fewer.
  - Rebound Rewind: 1.5 per Short vs 4.
  - The three explainer channels together: 1 vs 3.
  - Our no-cards rule holds.
- **Opening:** hits are as calm as misses or calmer.
  - The viral formats in the survey hold one clip: most of their top Shorts have 1 shot in the first 3 s and 1-7 shots in total.
  - breakawayclips' hits run 8-12 s on 3-4 shots.
  - **Let a great clip run.**
- **Pace after the opening:** mixed. CoachClank's hits cut faster (an average shot of 2.6 s vs 3.8 s); the other channels show no gap. **No rule.**
- **Length within a channel: no difference** (Swishyyy 18 vs 20 s, Hoopiverse 27 vs 28, CoachClank 22 vs 21, Rebound Rewind 74 vs 78).
  - Each format has its own length: moments 15-30 s, explainers and lists 60-80 s, one clip plus a caption 5-12 s, elimination games 25 s.
  - *Correction:* on Oct 27 I told the owner that hit scripts were shorter. That came from mixing channels, and within one channel it doesn't hold.

## 4. Scripts (86 NBA, 36 soccer)
- **Line 1 (NBA):** a bold claim or the scene beats a number or a question.
  - Line 1 with a number: 11% of hits vs 23% of misses.
  - A question: 15% vs 21%.
  - Ahmed's Goal opens with a question in both groups (55%), because it reads its title aloud.
- **The ending (NBA):** a twist or a punchline in 64% of hits (28 of 44) vs 52% of misses.
  - Emotional endings, number endings and open-question endings: 3 hits vs 11 misses.
- **Tone (NBA):** dramatic or sentimental in 8 hits vs 14 misses.
- **No follow or subscribe ask in any hit.**
- **Speed:** about 3.8 words a second (about 225 wpm) in both groups.
- **Script library** (`Script Library (2026-10-27).md`): the 15 best NBA and 5 best soccer scripts, for their shape.
  - The best ones state the strange thing in sentence 1 ("This is the biggest basketball hoop in the world").
  - Then they turn ("But somehow, almost everyone who tries it...").
  - Then they end on a punchline or a bigger number ("...a shot worth 1,000 points").

## 5. What changes for us
1. **NBA lanes:**
   - moments that pass the stranger test (spectacle, size, kids, funny);
   - a list with a clever frame, weekly: the countdown test starts now ("at every age", "players who...", "3 times [star] did X");
   - news only when it's a spectacle you can watch. **No "Is X about to...?" or "are they doomed?" takes.**
2. **Soccer:**
   - megastar drama and superlatives ("Transfers that completely backfired", "Craziest...");
   - not speculative transfer or club questions, and not lists of lesser-known names.
3. **Scripts:**
   - line 1 is the strange thing stated plainly (for NBA, not a number or a question);
   - turn early;
   - end on a twist or a punchline;
   - skip sentimental endings;
   - never ask for a follow.
4. **Editing:** no cards, hold the opening, and let a great clip run. No new cutting-pace rule.
