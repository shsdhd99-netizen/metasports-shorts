# Soccer Script Craft (Nov 1, 2026)

Why four of our FC Shorts got 15-30K and the other eight got 1-3K, line by line, plus what the competitor data adds. Read with `Soccer Topic Study (Nov 1, 2026).md`. Tiers: [V] measured, [S] small sample, [H] guess.

## 1. The surprise: the script template is NOT what separates them
Every Short, hit or flop, uses the same template: title restated as line 1, "Back in [year]...", a turn every 8-12 s, "But here's the wild part" near the end, an ironic last line. S02, S04, S06 and S08 all follow it as closely as S01 and S03 do. **The structure is a constant, so it cannot explain a 10x gap [V, n=12].** What differs is the premise.

## 2. Hits vs flops, line by line
| | Hits: S01 Lehmann, S03 Dudek, S05 Pickford, S17 Krul | Flops: S02, S04, S06, S08, S15, S16, S18 |
|---|---|---|
| **What the viewer already knows** | Little. Few people know a keeper read a list in his sock, danced on his line by plan, or was swapped in for the shootout | A lot. Zidane's headbutt, Hand of God, Lewandowski's five goals and Coutinho's fee are all famous: the viewer thinks "I know this" in 3 s |
| **Hidden information** | Always a secret: "Lehmann had a secret", "Pickford had a secret", "Van Gaal did something that stunned the whole stadium", Carragher's idea | Rarely. The hook is an event, not a secret |
| **The device** | One tangible thing you can picture: a page in a sock, notes on a water bottle, a dance, a substitution | A result, a record or a number |
| **Stakes** | A World Cup / Champions League / final shootout, so every kick is yes/no | Often a league game or a transfer: no kick-by-kick tension |
| **The undercut** | The trick is exposed or fails mid-story (S01: "only two players on his list took a penalty"; S17: Krul had saved 2 of 20; S05: England missed the third kick) | Rare |
| **The last line** | A reversal of the opening device: the note sold for a million, the hero played 12 games, the bottle went to Messi, Van Gaal had no subs left | Mostly a number or an "and then he got paid" line |
| **Numbers** | Few and round | S15 carries 160M, 8.5M, 120M, 20M, 8-2, 1946 in one Short; S06 has 5 goals, 3:22, 58 s, 8:59, 6, 1977, 4 records |
| **Timelines** | One, plus one flash-forward at the end | S16 and S18 run two timelines (2004 pizza + last week; 2010 sprinklers + today) |
| **Person** | Famous keeper but the *odd thing* is the title | Star name + known event |

Words: hits run 190-265 words (S17 ~200 is the tightest and got 16.6K in 54 h). Flops run 230-310.

## 3. Competitor evidence (see Topic Study)
- Goalkeeper stories are 1.30x a channel's own median pooled, 1.29x without Ahmed [V]. Their best are one-object stories: "Every Match New Gloves for Donnarumma" (4.4M, 176x), "The Chillest Goalkeeper" Szczesny (26M), "The True Definition of a Goalkeeper" (12M). The pattern is "an odd habit of a goalkeeper", the same as our hits.
- Titles that start "When...", "What..." or promise "the real reason" beat plain titles [S]. Name-led, number-led and "most/worst" titles are below average [V].
- Under 45 s is clearly worse [V]; our 45-59 s is right.
- Fan/kid and family stories rank high channel-by-channel (1.53 balanced). We have zero of them posted [H: worth 2 tests].

## 4. Do and don't
**Do**
1. Pick a premise where the viewer does NOT know the answer, ideally a goalkeeper with one odd habit/device in a high-stakes shootout.
2. Put the odd thing in the title ("...hid a cheat sheet in his sock"). The star is the second line.
3. Name ONE object or act the viewer can picture, and show it in the first 4 s of footage.
4. Include one undercut mid-story (the trick almost fails, or is exposed).
5. End by flipping the opening device (the thing he relied on becomes the irony).
6. Keep numbers to 3 or fewer, all round or spoken as words; one score and one year at most.
7. One timeline. A flash-forward is allowed at the end only.
8. 190-250 words, 45-59 s.

**Don't**
1. Don't open with a famous event the audience already knows (the Zidane headbutt, Hand of God) unless the odd detail is in the title.
2. Don't build on money: fee, salary, auction price as the payoff (S15, S08) [S, n=2 posted].
3. Don't lead with a star's name when the oddity is a person's habit.
4. Don't run two timelines (S16, S18).
5. Don't use a list of records or times (S06).
6. Don't promise "the wild part" if it's only another number.

## 5. Template (goalkeeper lane)
1. **Line 1 (title):** "Goalkeepers who [odd thing]." (series title; the story is one person).
2. **Setup (2-3 sentences):** "Back in [year], [team] played [team] in [big match]." Stakes: it went to penalties.
3. **The secret/device (2 sentences):** what he did, one concrete object.
4. **Turn 1 (undercut):** the trick is exposed or half-works.
5. **The decisive kick (3-4 sentences):** one name per kick, binary outcomes.
6. **"But here's the wild part" (2 sentences):** the reversal of the device. No money line, no promo.

## 6. One example script (sourced, goalkeeper lane) - DRAFT, to be fact-checked before voice
Source notes: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_(footballer,_born_1976)), [FourFourTwo](https://www.fourfourtwo.com/features/people-probably-werent-happy-with-me-ricardo-on-breaking-english-hearts-twice), [Sky](https://skysports.com/football/news/19692/12012521/darius-vassell-on-englands-golden-generation-and-his-penalty-miss-at-euro-2004). Sources disagree on whether the gloves came off for Vassell's kick or the last one, so the script says only that the gloves were off for the penalty he saved and the one he scored. Anything not in the notes (venue, the other kicks, Beckham's miss) is deliberately left out.

> Goalkeepers who took their gloves off to save a penalty. Back in 2004, Portugal played England in the quarter-final of the Euros, and it finished {2-2|two-all}. So it went to penalties. Portugal's goalkeeper, Ricardo, did something no one expected. For the penalty he saved, he took off his gloves. Bare hands, against a professional taker. Darius Vassell stepped up. Ricardo dived to his left, and saved it. Then, when Portugal needed one more, Ricardo walked up himself. Still with no gloves. He put the ball down, and he scored past David James. {6-5|Six-five}. Portugal went through. But here's the wild part. Years later, Vassell and Ricardo were playing golf together, and Ricardo finally told him why he'd taken his gloves off.

**Check before voicing:** every sentence above is in the sources, except that the exact kick the gloves came off for differs by outlet (the line says "the penalty he saved", true under both). Sources say: gloves off, saved Vassell's, scored the winner past James, 6-5 after 2-2, Vassell said Ricardo told him why while golfing. The undercut (Rule 4) is missing here: the real one is that England had gone out on penalties before, and a bare-handed save should have been harder; add only after a source for the England shootout record is in notes.md.
