## 3. How they're edited (from the auto-frames) [S]
- **The picture is the event**, whether game broadcast, fan phone video, warm-up or creator clip.
  - The three auto-frames are usually different angles or moments of the same event (52 of the 60 newest).
  - They cut between angles and replays of one thing, not to filler.
- **No cards, no stat graphics, no numbers written over the picture.** The broadcast's own scorebug is the only "data".
- **Captions:** white bold italic ALL CAPS, black outline, 2-3 words, centred mid-frame. Their earliest Shorts used coloured words and are their weakest (age-confounded).
- **Wide broadcast footage:** zoomed in, or set over a blurred copy of itself (like our `blurfit`).
- **Narrated:** the captions read like a voiceover ("BUT WHEN STEPH...", "SO ACCURATE THAT..."). Voice type and music: unknown until someone listens [H].

## 5. What we change (applied to the skills today)
1. **NBA lane priority:** about 2 of every 3 NBA Shorts are a **moment you can watch**:
   - last night's funny, odd or skilful moment from a game, warm-up, bench, fan, mascot or kid;
   - or a viral creator/streetball clip with an NBA name in it.
   Stat and record paradoxes (THE #42 FORMAT, #69-style number gaps) drop to about 1 in 3, and only with a household-name star.
2. **The clip carries it:**
   - open on the moment;
   - show it from every angle the source has (live, replay, reverse);
   - the narration adds who, why and what happened next.
3. **Title:** a short statement pointing at the moment ("He Pretended To Be Klay Thompson"). No new rule on emoji or questions (no effect in their data or ours).
4. **Sources:** X posts of last night's moments (NBA, teams, House of Highlights, ClutchPoints) through `Tools/x_clip.py`, plus team sites (`Tools/nba_videos.py`).
   - Copy-check every topic against Swishyyy's titles as well as the bigger channels. Never retell their exact Short.
   - Their list is in this note's data file `Swishyyy Shorts (Oct 26, 2026).csv`.
