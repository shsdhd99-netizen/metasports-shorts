# Shorts mechanics and policy (Oct 24, 2026)

**Scope.** This note covers how YouTube Shorts distribution works in 2025-26, what makes a Short travel, the policy risk of our setup (AI narration over real broadcast and archive footage, with burned-in captions), and how Shorts pay. It applies to both channels: MetaSports (NBA explainers, 20-30 s) and MetaSports FC (soccer story Shorts, 55-60 s). It does not cover ElevenLabs pricing, posting times or footage sources, because another agent has those.

**Method.**
- **Sources, in this order:** YouTube Help pages and the YouTube Blog (read directly today), TeamYouTube and Creator Insider statements, trade press quoting YouTube staff, then data studies and creator reports.
- **Our own data:** the Studio exports in `Research Notes/Studio exports/`, read today.
- **Not reachable from this cloud machine:**
  - **Reddit is blocked**, both directly and through search, so no r/NewTubers or r/PartneredYoutube threads were read. Creator reports here come from press coverage of creators, the YouTube Help Community, and our own data.
  - **YouTube video pages hit a bot check**, so Creator Insider videos are quoted from press transcripts, not watched.
- **Overlap:** `Monetization Research (Oct 23, 2026).md` already covers the YPP thresholds and the policy basics. This note adds what changed in Sep-Oct 2026, the algorithm and retention evidence, and a verdict.

**Tiers**
- **[V]:** official YouTube wording, or measured in our own data.
- **[S]:** several credible sources agree.
- **[H]:** a single source, an anecdote, a vendor claim, or my inference.

## Top 7 findings

### 1. The stay-or-swipe test in the first second decides almost everything, and our numbers match it exactly
- **What.** A Short isn't chosen from a thumbnail. It starts playing in the feed, and YouTube watches whether people stay or swipe away.
  - The first few hundred to few thousand views are a test on a small group of viewers.
  - A Short that loses that test "won't go much beyond those initial, impression-like views". A Short that wins is shown to tens of thousands, or more.
  - Each Short is judged on its own. A flop doesn't drag down the channel or the next upload.
- **Evidence.**
  - Rene Ritchie, YouTube Blog, Apr 2024: Shorts use "view vs swiped away" instead of impressions and click-through. "Most thumbnails are never seen because people are in the feed" (Shorts lead Todd Sherman, Creator Insider, Aug 2023).
  - YouTube Help defines the metric as "the percentage of times that viewers viewed your Shorts versus swiped away". Studio now labels it "Stayed to watch" (it's the column name in our exports).
  - Todd Beaupré (YouTube's director of growth and discovery, Creator Insider, Sep 1 2026): early tests reach "people who've never watched the channel before", and "no metric on its own is a good indicator of value".
  - **Our data:**
    - 80-87% stayed gets 20-30K views. That's NBA #42 (86.9%, 19.4K), #48 (83.3%, 22.1K) and FC S01 (80.4%, 30.1K).
    - 56-65% stayed stalls at 1-4K. That's #52 (59.1%), #63 (57.6%), FC S02 (56.2%, 1.3K) and S03 (64.7%, 1.6K).
    - The middle band (72-77%) lands at 5-10K: #64, #36 and #49. #58 (75%, 23.3K) is the exception.
- **Tier:** [V] for the mechanism and our numbers. YouTube publishes no pass mark, so the 75-80% bar is ours [H].
- **Do.**
  - Treat "Stayed to watch" as the number to beat. Read it at 24-48 h. Aim for 78%+ on NBA and 75%+ on FC (FC Shorts are longer, so a slightly lower bar is fair).
  - Spend most of the editing effort on frame 1 and the first spoken line. The rest of the Short only matters once that test is passed.

### 2. A Short makes its views in about 48 hours; the "revival weeks later" story is rare, and recency counts for more since September 2025
- **What.** Almost all of a Short's views arrive in the first two or three days. YouTube says a weak Short "may blow up another day", but it gives no mechanism.
  - Since mid-September 2025, large channels report that Shorts older than about 28-30 days get far fewer impressions.
  - The one late surge we've seen came within days, not weeks: #48.
- **Evidence.**
  - **Our Studio daily views [V]:**
    - #42: 17,016 on day 1, then 2,269 on day 2, then 33, 19, 5, 12 and 5.
    - FC S01: 27,798 on day 1, then 217.
    - #58: 88% of its views in its first 2 days.
    - #48 is the exception. It was posted late on Oct 20 (UTC): 25 views on its first day, then 3,707, then 14,577, then 3,761. So early numbers aren't final for the first 2-3 days.
  - **YouTube:** Ritchie, Apr 2024: "(though it may blow up another day)". Beaupré, Sep 2026: "We're not basing decisions just on those initial moments."
  - **Freshness shift:**
    - Mario Joos (retention strategist) on 7 channels with 100M-1B monthly views, and Tim Chesney (about 2B lifetime views): evergreen Shorts "simply tanked" from Sep 2025.
    - Reported by SEJ, Tubefilter and PPC Land, Dec 2025.
    - YouTube hasn't confirmed it.
- **Tier:** [V] for our data and YouTube's wording; [S] for the freshness shift.
- **Do.**
  - Judge a Short at 48-72 h, then move on. Don't plan around a flop coming back.
  - To retry a topic, make a **new cut**: a new first frame, first line and angle.
  - **Never delete and re-upload the same file.** Sherman: "There is a risk there that that gets seen as spam in our systems."
  - Evergreen topics still work, but each one gets its main chance in its first days.

### 3. NEW on Oct 1, 2026: narration that describes what's on screen no longer counts as original, and channels that mostly re-upload get less Shorts reach
- **What.** On Oct 1, 2026 YouTube changed the Shorts recommendation system to "further prioritize original content and reduce the reach of content re-uploaded from other creators without adding anything of your own."
  - Channels "primarily aggregating or re-uploading other creators' videos without significant changes will likely see less distribution within the Shorts feed."
  - In the Creator Insider video released with it, Ritchie said value must be added, "not just VO descriptions of what's happening on screen, minor technical edits, or template-based bulk changes."
  - It affects reach, not pay. Pay still runs under the reused-content rule.
  - A channel that moves toward original content "will re-evaluate reach". There's no timeline and no Studio flag that shows whether a channel was affected.
- **Evidence.** The TeamYouTube post in the YouTube Help Community, signed "JJ (TeamYouTube)", read in full today. Ritchie quoted by Search Engine Journal and PPC Land.
- **Tier:** [V].
- **Do.**
  - **This is the rule that matters most for a channel like ours.** Every script has to carry something the footage doesn't show by itself: a cause, a number, a comparison, a rule, history, or a verdict.
  - Run the two tests in the Policy section (the audio-only test and the mute test) on every Short.
  - The "15-25 s moments" lane in the nba-short skill, where a clip is narrated as it happens, is the most exposed format we have. Retire it, or give every moment a "why" or a record.

### 4. Our format is on the allowed side of the reused-content rule, but only while every Short tells its own story
- **What.** YouTube's monetization policy lists these as allowed:
  - "Replays of a sports tournament where you explain the moves a competitor did to succeed"
  - edited footage with a new storyline or commentary
  - a scene with "rewritten the dialog and changed the voiceover"
- It lists these as not allowed:
  - clips with little narrative
  - compilations
  - "Content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create" (website text, news feeds)
  - template-made content ("inauthentic content", renamed from "repetitious" on Jul 15, 2025)
- **The July 16, 2026 clarification** split inauthentic content into three groups:
  1. Generic, repetitive or template-based content: "characters... put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome", or AI content from "generic or unoriginal templates".
  2. Off-putting content that "mimics existing formats or stories to a degree that the videos feel interchangeable".
  3. AI personas posing as experts on health, legal, financial or political topics.
- A recurring series format is fine "as long as each video has its own storyline".
- **Evidence:** Help 1311392 (read today); Matt Halprin (YouTube's head of trust and safety) via TechCrunch, Jul 20 2026; Tubefilter, Jul 13 2026; Ritchie via SEJ, Jul 2025: "There are no changes to YouTube's reused content policies on clips, compilations, reaction content."
- **Tier:** [V].
- **Do.**
  - Never script from a single article; that reads as "readings of other materials". Each script combines at least two sources plus our own angle.
  - Rotate 3-4 structures per channel.
  - On FC, make the Ahmed-style format our own: our own openers and turns, not "title read aloud, then Back in [year]" every time.

### 5. The 60-second rule is still the safe line, but YouTube's own Help pages now contradict each other
- **What.** The US-English pages say this:
  - **Over 1 minute:** "Any Short that is over one minute in duration with an active copyright claim of any type... will be blocked globally". It isn't playable, recommended or monetized until the claim is resolved.
  - **Under 1 minute:** "no changes in limitations". The claimant's own setting applies, which can be monetize, track or block, by country.
- The UK, India and Australia versions of the same page now say something different: "Starting on 24 September 2026, new Shorts longer than one minute (but under three minutes) with an active Content ID claim will no longer be automatically blocked and may remain playable."
- The Shorts monetization policy, in both US and UK English, still says Shorts over one minute "that contain claimed content are blocked and not eligible for monetization."
- **Evidence:** Help 15424877 (en vs en-GB, en-IN, en-AU, fetched today side by side); Help 12504220; Help 6013276.
- **Tier:** [V] for the wording on each page. What actually happens to a 61-90 s claimed Short today is [H] (uncertain).
- **Do.**
  - **FC hard cap: 59.5 s.** S02 (64 s) and S03 (71 s) broke it.
  - Even if the UK wording is the new rule, "may remain playable" doesn't promise recommendation or pay.
  - NBA, at 20-30 s, isn't affected.

### 6. AI narration is allowed and needs no AI label; realistic AI images of real people or events do. Never clone a real person's voice.
- **What needs disclosure.** YouTube requires disclosure only for realistic AI content that:
  - "Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do"
  - "Alters footage of a real event or place"
  - "Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur"
  - or is AI music that's the main focus
- **The Help page's own examples** include "AI generated realistic videos of a match between two real professional tennis players".
- **No disclosure needed:** "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs", scripts, captions, and voice or audio repair. A generic AI narrator who isn't imitating anyone isn't listed either way. On that wording, no disclosure is needed [V wording / H reading].
- **Labels cost nothing.** "Disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." Ritchie, May 2026: labels "do not affect how our videos are recommended or whether they can earn money."
- **What changed in 2026:**
  - **Since May 27, 2026:** a prominent label (an overlay on Shorts) for photorealistic AI, and automatic labelling when YouTube detects significant photorealistic AI use.
  - **Since Oct 2025:** likeness detection for faces and voices, expanded at Made on YouTube on Sep 23, 2026.
  - **ElevenLabs** embeds an inaudible watermark in its audio. YouTube may label "non-music AI-generated audio" in the expanded description only.
- **Evidence:** Help 14328491 (read today); YouTube Blog, May 27 2026; TechCrunch, Oct 21 2025; ElevenLabs Help (watermarking).
- **Tier:** [V].
- **Do.**
  - Use the account-owned "MetaSports Designed Narrator v1" or the stock voice Alex.
  - Retire the "pham" clone unless its original speaker is the owner, or has given written consent. Its source is "the old channel narration", and the Oct 19 audition found its speaker can't verify the voice.
  - Answer "No" to AI disclosure for narrated real footage.
  - Answer "Yes" for any realistic AI-made image or video of a real player or event, such as a recreated historical scene.

### 7. Shorts pay by engaged views, and at today's pace the gate matters, not the rate per view
- **How the pool works.** Shorts ad money is pooled each month and shared by each creator's share of eligible **engaged views**. Monetized creators keep **45%** of their allocation.
- **What isn't paid:**
  - **Music:** Shorts using tracks from YouTube's music partners split their pool share with those partners.
  - **Claimed content:** non-music claimed content isn't "credited as making a contribution". What happens to our share when a league claims a Short under 60 s isn't stated [H].
  - **Ineligible views:** non-original Shorts ("unedited clips from others' movies or TV shows") earn nothing.
- **Engaged vs public views.** YPP eligibility and pay both use engaged views, not the public counter. "Clicking to watch or watching past the initial seconds" (YouTube Blog, Aug 19 2026).
  - On our NBA channel, engaged views were **54% of public views** (prior note, Sep 16-Oct 14 export).
- **Rates:** typical Shorts RPM is $0.03-$0.20 per 1,000 views. A US-heavy audience reaches about $0.33.
- **The gate:**
  - **Our pace:** about 17K public views a day, which is about 0.84M engaged views per 90 days.
  - **Full YPP:** the bar is 10M qualified Shorts views per 90 days, and **20M for new applicants from Feb 1, 2027**.
  - **Payout floor:** from Feb 1, 2027, Shorts pay pauses for any channel below 10M qualified views per 90 days.
  - So we're about 12x short of the bar today, and about 24x short of the 2027 bar.
- **Evidence:** Help 12504220 (read today); YouTube Blog, Aug 2026; AIR Media-Tech (274 channels) and vidIQ for RPM; our export.
- **Tier:** [V] for the rules and our ratio; [S] for the RPM ranges. Sports-only RPM data: none found.
- **Do.**
  - Optimise for "Stayed to watch" and engaged views. Ignore the public counter when tracking YPP progress.
  - Shorts ad money will be small even once we're in. At our current pace it would be roughly $100-$1,100 a year [H].
  - Treat YPP status as the prize: it unlocks the invite-only Shorts bonus pilot (announced Oct 16, 2026) and fan funding.

## Policy risk: AI voice + broadcast footage

### Verdict
**Allowed, and monetizable in principle. The risk sits in how individual Shorts are built, not in the setup itself.**

- No rule bans AI narration or treats it as a red flag. A generic or owned AI voice needs no label.
- Sports footage with a voice that "explains the moves" is YouTube's own example of acceptable reused content.

**What can hurt us, from most to least likely:**
1. **Shorts where the voice just retells the clip.** These lose Shorts reach under the Oct 1, 2026 update, and they're "non-original" for pay.
2. **A templated look.** That means a very common stock voice (Alex is the most-used voice in the ElevenLabs library), the same structure every day, 2 channels from one pipeline, and FC copying Ahmed's format. That's what the July 2026 "inauthentic content" language describes.
3. **Copyright.** Claims are routine and mostly harmless. Strikes (formal takedowns) are dangerous: 3 in 90 days ends the channel and linked channels. Soccer Shorts over 60 s are blocked.
4. **Channel hygiene at YPP review.** Reviewers start with the most-viewed and newest videos. Our most-viewed list still holds old bare-moment Shorts and off-niche Sora/AI meme Shorts, including realistic AI Snoop Dogg (prior note).

**By channel:**
- **NBA:** the format is low-to-medium risk. The NBA has long tolerated highlight channels: Adam Silver in 2015, "for the most part highlights are marketing", and the league said in 2020 its approach hadn't changed. That was 2020; nothing newer was found [S].
- **FC:** medium risk. Soccer rights are split between leagues and broadcasters in each country, and I found no current data on how they claim Shorts [H]. The FC format also copies a single competitor closely.

### The rules in one table
| Rule | What it says (official) | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| Reused content (Help 1311392) | Allowed: sports replays where you explain the moves; edited footage with a new storyline or commentary. Not allowed: clips with little narrative, compilations, reading others' material | Our explainer and story Shorts qualify **if** the script adds the explanation. Applies to the whole channel, and even with permission |
| Inauthentic content (renamed Jul 15 2025; clarified Jul 16 2026) | Template-made, same situation and same outcome, interchangeable formats, generic AI templates. A series is fine when each video has its own storyline | A daily "Who has missed the most X?" series is fine while each one has its own story and payoff. Churning near-identical versions isn't |
| Shorts originality update (Oct 1 2026) | Less reach for channels that mostly re-upload or aggregate. Voice-over describing the screen, minor edits and template changes don't count as original | Biggest new risk to reach. Applies to the Shorts feed only |
| Shorts revenue (Help 12504220) | Pay from eligible engaged views. Non-original Shorts are ineligible; claimed Shorts over 1 min are blocked | Unedited or barely narrated clips earn nothing even after YPP |
| Content ID (Help 6013276, 15424877) | Claims: the owner can block, monetize or track, by country. No strike. Over 1 min plus an active claim means blocked (US page; UK/IN/AU pages say this changed on Sep 24 2026) | NBA 20-30 s: claims mean lost revenue or some lost countries at worst. FC: stay under 60 s |

| AI disclosure (Help 14328491; Blog May 27 2026) | Disclose realistic AI depictions of real people or events. Own-voice cloning and production help are exempt. Labels don't affect reach or pay | Generic or designed narrator over real footage: no label. AI recreations of real players: label |

### What creators report in practice
- **No documented case specific to AI-voice sports story channels was found** [H: an absence of evidence, partly because Reddit was unreachable].
- **Jan 2026:** automated and faceless channels, and some networks of channels at once, were demonetized for "inauthentic content". Most were AI automation setups running 10+ near-identical channels. Some creators who script and narrate their own work were also hit. Some appeals with human review succeeded, such as a Warhammer lore channel. YouTube didn't confirm a sweep (PiunikaWeb, Jan 12 2026) [S].
- **Sep 2025:** big Shorts channels lost reach on back-catalogue Shorts (finding 2) [S].
- **Oct 2026 originality update:** too new for outcome data. No reach-drop reports yet (AITuber, Oct 3 2026) [H].
- **Proof that the format can travel:** the AI-voiced NBA story channel CoachClank (432 Shorts) and Ahmed's soccer network (1.3B views) both reach millions with AI narration over footage (our earlier studies). Whether they're monetized can't be seen from public data [H].

### What makes our Shorts "transformative" enough (the working test)
YouTube's test is whether viewers can see "a meaningful difference between the original video and your video". Since Oct 1, 2026, a voice that only retells the clip fails it. Run three checks on every script and cut:

1. **Audio-only test.** Play the voice track with no picture. It has to hold up as a story or argument, with at least one thing **you can't see in the clip**: a number, a record, a cause ("because..."), history, a rule, a comparison or a verdict.
   - Fail: "Curry pulls up from 30 feet and drains it."
   - Pass: "That's his 4,000th three. Nobody else has 3,000."
2. **Mute test** (from PPC Land / Outlier Kit). Mute the voice and imagine our captions and cards removed. If what's left is just someone else's clip, the voice and captions are all we added, so they must carry the story (test 1).
3. **Source mix.** At least two footage sources per Short, plus our own cards, screenshots or graphics. Never use another creator's edit, watermark or re-narration as the base (S06's "Narração Retrô" reel is exactly this case).
   - This doesn't make a Short original; YouTube says technical edits don't count. It just keeps it from looking like a re-upload.

### Safeguards (do these)
1. **Script gate:** every Short passes the audio-only test before voicing. Add it to the critic and QA checklists.
2. **Retire or rework the "moments" lane** (15-25 s clips narrated as they happen). Every Short needs a why, a record or a verdict.
3. **FC length cap: 59.5 s** in `Tools/qa_short.py` as a hard fail. NBA stays at 20-30 s.
4. **No other creators' material as the base.** Use only league, team or broadcaster sources, archive.org, and our own screenshots and graphics. Crop out no watermarks; skip any clip that has one.
5. **Voice:** use only the Designed Narrator or Alex.
   - Retire the "pham" clone unless consent is documented.
   - Never a sound-alike of a commentator or player.
   - Consider making the owned Designed Narrator the house voice on at least one channel, so the two channels don't sound like thousands of others [H].
6. **AI disclosure:** "No" for narrated real footage; "Yes" for any realistic AI image or video of a real person or event. Log the answer in UPLOAD.md.
7. **Claims:** read Studio's restrictions column at 48 h and log every claim (claimant, policy, countries) in the Outcome Log.
   - Never dispute a league or broadcaster claim without a written fair-use reason.
   - If a claimant blocks repeatedly, stop using that claimant's footage.
8. **Vary the template:**
   - At least 3 structures in rotation per channel.
   - No more than 2 of the same format in a row.
   - No identical title patterns back to back.
   - FC writes its own openers and doesn't copy Ahmed's lines word for word.
9. **Keep proof of human work:** sources.csv, notes.md, and the script drafts per Short (already in Productions). That's the material for a YPP appeal video, which runs under 5 minutes and shows how we make the content.
10. **Before applying for YPP:** set to private (don't delete) the off-niche Sora/AI Shorts and the bare-moment old Shorts. Rewrite the About section to describe the research, scripting and editing process.
11. **Every fact checked** (already in our pipeline). False claims about real people can create separate policy problems, and they cost trust.

## How Shorts distribution works (detail for question 1)

**Signals.**
- **What YouTube says about recommendations in general:** it uses "viewing behaviour, likes, dislikes, subscriptions and feedback, including from satisfaction surveys" (How YouTube Works) [V].
- **On Shorts specifically:** the stay/swipe decision is the first gate (finding 1) [V].
- **Beaupré, Sep 2026:**
  - "No metric on its own is a good indicator of value."
  - YouTube doesn't "assume that all time is valuable"; surveys separate time spent from satisfaction.
  - Subscriptions matter less than creators think: "even your best videos, 90% of the time, your subscribed audience isn't deciding to watch" [V, via PPC Land].
- **Likes, comments, shares, loops, subscribes:** no official weights exist. Vendor blogs that rank "loop rate" or "share rate" cite no YouTube source [H].
- **Negative feedback:** since Jun 25-26, 2026 there's no dislike button on Shorts. The like is now a heart, and the remaining negative signals are "Not interested" and "Don't recommend this channel" (Engadget) [S]. A title the video doesn't pay off risks a whole-channel "Don't recommend" [H].
- **Repeat impressions:** in one experiment, a viewer "wouldn't be recommended a video more than eight times" (Beaupré) [V via press]. So one viewer isn't shown the same Short over and over.

**Seed testing.**
- "Find a set of people that may enjoy your video", then traffic either grows or tapers off (Sherman, 2023) [V].
- Early viewers aren't only subscribers (Beaupré, 2026) [V].
- Formats are ranked by separate systems: "We haven't seen evidence of Shorts negatively impacting long-form performance" (YouTube Blog, May 2025) [V].

**Why a Short stalls at 1-2K.**
- It lost the first test: viewers swiped, or left early, at a higher rate than the Shorts it competed with.
- Our 56-65% stayed group stalls at 1-4K, which matches [V].
- **What it isn't:**
  - There are no fixed "300-view" or "2K" walls and no channel penalty [S].
  - It's not a shadowban. Our own channel has 20K hits and 1K flops in the same week [V].
- **Other causes, rarer:**
  - The Short was blocked or never entered the feed (over 60 s with a claim, or a wrong aspect ratio). Check "Shown in feed" first.
  - Another Short of the same game moment is winning the same audience [H].
  - Two of our own Shorts posted the same day (S01 and S02) [H].

**Revival.** See finding 2. Official: "may blow up another day". In practice, a second wave can come within 2-3 days (#48). After about a week a Short is close to flat, and since Sep 2025 big channels report Shorts older than about 30 days fade faster.

**Titles, hashtags, thumbnails.**
- **Hashtags:** "Are they required? No, of course not" (Sherman) [V].
- **Hashtag correlation:** in Metricool's 2026 study (800K videos), Shorts with hashtags averaged about 10% fewer views and about 9% more engagement. That's a correlation [H].
- **Thumbnails:**
  - Custom Shorts thumbnails arrived in July 2026 for YPP channels, desktop only, with no A/B testing [S].
  - They matter for Search, channel pages and suggestions, not the feed [V].
- **Titles:**
  - In the feed, the title shows as a small line of text over the video. No official source says titles change feed ranking. They help YouTube and Search understand the topic [H/S].
  - Our rule (title = the spoken line 1) is fine. The first frame and first line do the selling.

|---|---|---|---|

No logos, black frames, scan lines or intro cards (already in qa_short) |

| Sound-off share on Shorts | No Shorts-specific number exists. Shorts play with sound on by default; since Jun 2026 muting takes two taps (stop the video, then mute). YouTube's 2016 claim of 96% sound-on is old and covers the whole platform | [H] | Design for sound on (the voice carries the story), but don't rely on audio for the first second |
| Loops and rewatches | Since Mar 31 2025 every replay is a public view, and replays push average % viewed past 100% (our #58: 104%; our NBA week average 97.6%). Competitor story channels run 1.9-3.2 s per shot (Round 9) | [H] | New information every few seconds, not cuts for their own sake (see Round 7) |
| Caption style | No evidence that the style moves views (Round 8) | [H] | Keep one clean house style; the words matter more than the styling |

Quso 2026 (108K Shorts, vendor): median views peak at 11-20 s. Beaupré: "puddles don't cause rain" and "often less is more". Sherman: "To what length do you need to tell your story?" **Ours:** FC S01 held 94% average viewed at 60 s; S03 held 72% at 71 s | [S]/[H] | Length follows the story. NBA 20-30 s, FC up to 59.5 s, but only if a turn lands about every 10 s |
| Music | "While trending sounds can increase discoverability, they're not essential" (YouTube Blog, May 2025). Partner music splits pool revenue. Commercial tracks add claims | [V] | Keep the quiet ElevenLabs bed; no label tracks over broadcast footage |
| Pre-publish check | "Get Feedback" (Sep 2026; US, 18+, YouTube mobile app): AI suggestions on hook, pacing and structure before publishing | [V] it exists; [H] useful | If the owner's account has it, run it on each upload as a free second opinion |

## Monetization (question 4)

- **How the money flows:** see finding 7 and `Monetization Research (Oct 23, 2026).md` for the full YPP table.
- **Engaged views vs views:**
  - **Engaged views decide eligibility and pay.** Since Aug 24, 2026 public views count from the first frame on every format. YouTube says the algorithm and YPP are unchanged by this [V].

## What sports Shorts channels commonly get wrong (question 5)

1. **Posting the clip and narrating it.** Since Oct 1, 2026 this loses reach, and it never earned money [V].
2. **Using other creators' edits:** House of Highlights-style reels, fan accounts, re-narrated reels, or anything with a watermark. That's exactly the "aggregating" the update targets [V].
3. **Going over 60 s with broadcast footage.** One claim blocks the Short (US wording) [V]. Our S02 and S03 did this.
4. **Reading the public view counter as YPP progress.** About half isn't engaged [V].
5. **Deleting and re-uploading flops, or reposting the same file.** YouTube warns this can be seen as spam. Make a new cut instead [V].
6. **Slow or unreadable first frames:** logos, black frames, soft archive close-ups, intro cards, or a hook that only works with sound [V ours, small n; S].
7. **Same-day pile-ups:** two Shorts on one game, or two of ours on the same day, competing for the same test audience [H].
8. **One template at volume across channels.** That's the "inauthentic content" pattern, and enforcement waves hit networks all at once [V policy; S enforcement].
9. **Disputing valid claims.** A rejected dispute can turn into a takedown, which is a strike [V].
10. **Off-niche or junk uploads among the most-viewed videos.** That's where reviewers look first [V].
11. **Clickbait the video doesn't pay off.** With no dislike button, "Don't recommend this channel" is the penalty viewers have left [H].
12. **Hashtag walls and chasing tags.** These have no official effect on the feed [S].
13. **AI recreations of real players or games without the AI label, or sound-alike voices of real commentators** [V policy].
14. **Waiting for a flop to come back** instead of re-cutting it [V ours].
