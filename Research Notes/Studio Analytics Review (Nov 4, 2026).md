## What the numbers say (research questions, not conclusions)
1. **The winning format is a goalkeeper doing something sneaky or odd** (cheat sheets, subbed on for a penalty, dancing on the line). Top four views are all goalkeepers; retention 83-96%.
2. **Subs come from one Short.** The sock cheat sheet gave 87 of 143 subs (61%); the water-bottle sequel gave only 8 despite equal views. The first of a pattern converts; the repeat does not. Question: what in the original (novelty, a named person, an ending) earns the follow?
3. **Manager and club-saga Shorts sit at 64-67% retention and about 3K views.** Test whether the cause is the hook or the topic.
4. **CTR does not predict views** (Loch Ness 6.5% got 2.5K; the sock Short 4.3% got 30K). Distribution is driven by retention in the feed.
5. **99% new viewers, 0% from subscribers:** no returning audience yet. A series title ("Goalkeepers who...") and a pinned "part 2" hook are the cheapest test.
6. **Possible 7-day lag:** the Nov 3 England subs Short (92% viewed) was still ramping at the time of the pull. Re-read at 48 h.
