## New findings (what we learned about what works)
1. **Only 2 of 20 clear the bar today (N1, F1), and F2 clears only if its total is sourced.** The binding constraints are originality and footage, not star power: 12 of 20 are TAKEN or CROWDED for the obvious phrasing.
2. **The same story can be TAKEN or FRESH depending on the hook.** "Mark Eaton 456 blocks" is CROWDED; "Mark Eaton car mechanic found" is FRESH. "Leicester found Mahrez Kante Vardy" is TAKEN; "Leicester title squad cost" is FRESH. The copy-check tool is matching hook words, so run it on the *angle* phrase, not the topic.
3. **Copy-check false positives exist.** Several TAKEN verdicts were triggered by an unrelated big video sharing words (a Mahrez tunnel-cam video, a Ronaldo Anfield tribute, HBO's Winning Time). Read the top-3 titles before trashing an idea.
4. **Money-gap and origin stories are the freshest lane** (Caicedo, Leicester, Duncan, Kobe, Cardiff all FRESH). Pure feats and moments (Klay 37, McGrady, Eaton, Bol) are the most told.
5. **Fees and numbers disagree between reliable sources** (Caicedo £4m BBC vs £4.5m ours; Vardy £1m vs £1.1m; Kante £5.6m Guardian vs £6m BBC). Our two-source rule needs one named source per number in the script.
6. **Official clip URLs are the slowest thing to find.** Only 3 official URLs were captured (Kobe draft, Magic Game 6, Klay's quarter on a likely-official channel). Most club YouTube pages return titles without watch URLs; pin them with `yt-dlp "ytsearch"` on the PC, not by web search.
7. **Goalkeeper stories still carry FC** (8x median) but the famous ones are all taken; the unfamiliar ones (Duckadam) are FRESH with weaker stranger scores.
