## 1. An Offensive Rebound Does Not Reset To Twenty-Four

- **First eight words:** "An offensive rebound does not reset to twenty-four."
- **Last line:** "It comes back at fourteen, and only because the shot hit the rim."
- **Second-0 picture:** A player securing an offensive rebound with both hands, the ball and the rim still in frame, and the broadcast shot clock already reading 14. Not a graphic card of the number. Not a defensive rebound.
- **Word count target:** 90–110.
- **Why it is a different family:** The visible fact is a shot-clock digit after a rebound. It is not a jersey number, a vote table, a height gap, a contract, a pick count, a referee-contact call, a star's day, a video game, or the lottery balls already cut.
- **What would kill it:** The 14 is not readable in the first second. The open is a defensive rebound, which really is 24. The script lists every other reset (loose-ball foul, out of bounds, flagrant) and becomes a fact stack. The last line switches to the airball while the loop is still the 14. A person opens a million-view Short that already says both the 14 and the rim condition.

**Turn.** The spoken "but" has to land before 40% of the words: it does not go back to 24, but it does come back, at 14, and only after rim contact.

**Six beats.** Not a script.

1. Rebound. The shot clock reads 14.
2. But that is the reset. It is not a new 24.
3. The 14 is only for the team that just shot it, after a miss that hit the ring, or a missed free throw that stayed live.
4. One contrast, then stop: an airball is not on that list. The clock is never reset when the shot fails to touch the rim.
5. Do not add the foul exceptions.
6. Return to the same rebound. Fourteen, because it hit the rim. That line has to be true on this picture.

**Sources.**

- Official NBA rule 7, opened Oct 10: "The shot clock shall be reset to 14 seconds anytime the following occurs: The offensive team is the first to gain possession after an unsuccessful free throw that remains in play, or an unsuccessful field goal attempt that contacts the basket ring." https://official.nba.com/rule-no-7-24-second-clock/
- Same page: "The shot clock is never reset on the following:" and the list includes "Field goal attempt which fails to touch the rim."

## 2. The Nearest Opponent Gets The Wrong-Hoop Basket

**Six beats.** Not a script.

**Sources.**

## 3. A Missed Free Throw Can Be Worth Two

- **First eight words:** "A missed free throw can be worth two."
- **Last line:** "Those two points belong to the player who tipped it, not the shooter."
- **Second-0 picture:** A free throw has already missed, the lane is still lined with bodies on the blocks, and a second player is tipping the ball through the net. The formation has to read as a free throw in that frame. A normal putback, with no lane lines and no bodies on the blocks, is the wrong picture.
- **Word count target:** 90–110.
- **Why it is a different family:** The visible fact is a tip through the net while the free-throw lane is still set, and the value is two instead of one. It is not the distance of the free-throw line (short 02, still unposted), not an underhand free throw, and not the shooter dunking his own free throw. Round 9 killed that dunk premise. The shooter is not the one scoring here.
- **What would kill it:** The frame looks like an ordinary putback. Six moving clips of a lined lane plus a tip do not exist. The script turns into when players may leave the blocks. That is a second rule, and it was not the sentence opened today. The open is a man standing at the line, which is the picture short 02 already could not illustrate. The clip is the shooter dunking his own attempt.

**Turn.** But the miss does not stay one point if somebody taps it in.

**Six beats.** Not a script.

1. The lane is still lined. The free throw has missed. A second player tips it through.
2. But a made free throw is one point. This is not that.
3. A miss that is tapped in counts two.
4. The two points are credited to the player who tapped it, not the shooter.
5. Do not add a lane-violation rule, a dunk, or a claim about whether the shooter is charged with a miss. Those sentences were not locked.
6. Return to the same tip, lane still lined. Two points. They belong to the tipper.

**Sources.**
