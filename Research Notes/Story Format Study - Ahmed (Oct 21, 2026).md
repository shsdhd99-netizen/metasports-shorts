# Story Format Study: why Ahmed's Shorts work (Oct 21, 2026)

**Owner's ask:** "Find out why ahmed is so successful at running multiple automation channels, what he does to have such viral shorts, engaging scripts, and good stories. make sure you can replicate a similar caption style, editing format, etc. do not try new things, stick with what works."

**Evidence used:**
- **Data:** full Shorts histories of Ahmed's Goal, Basketball and Business (3,154 Shorts; `Research Notes/Ahmed Network Data (Oct 21, 2026)/`).
- **Transcripts:** 19 scripts.
  - 12 YouTube captions: Goal top 6, middle 2, bottom 3; plus Business 2.
  - 7 Whisper transcripts of TikTok copies.
  - The earlier Basketball study (`Competitor Script Breakdown (Oct 15, 2026)`).
- **Watched frame by frame:** 7 Shorts via their TikTok copies (`Tools/watch_short.py`): 6 Goal, 1 Basketball.
- **Measurements:** captions measured on full-size frames; audio measured for loudness and noise floor.

**Evidence tiers:**
- [V] measured on their files or data;
- [S] seen in a few of them;
- [H] my reading, not proven.

## 1. Why the network works (the business side)
1. **One format, run on three audiences.**
   - The same title templates and script shape across soccer, NBA and business. 121 concepts are word-for-word shared between soccer and NBA. [V]
   - A title that works becomes a series: "Episode 2…12" on 126 NBA and 141 soccer titles; 7 Business Shorts share one title. [V]
2. **Volume with a steady rhythm.** About 2 a day per channel for 18+ months, batch-scheduled in fixed UTC windows. [V]
   - When they pushed to about 3 a day (Jul-Sep 2026), medians fell and they pulled back. [V]
3. **Evergreen stories, not news.** Every Short works months later, so the back catalogue keeps earning. [V]
4. **Several ways to earn:**
   - YouTube ads;
   - TikTok, where every video runs past 1 minute; [V]
   - in-script sponsors: a Polymarket mention inside the Curaçao Short, tagged #PolyPartner on TikTok; [V]
   - YouTube auto-dubbing into other languages. [V]
5. **Cross-posting:** the same file goes to YouTube, TikTok and Instagram. [V]

## 3. The script (same on all 3 channels)
| Beat | When | What it does | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Title read aloud** | 0.0-3.5 s | Line 1 is the title, word for word ("Players who refused to play in matches."). The promise. | 19 of 19 [V] |
| **"Back in [year], [famous name]…"** | 3-10 s | Time, place, the name and the stakes in one sentence | 12 of 19 start "Back in…" or "The year was…" [V] |
| **Turn 1** | about 10-17 s | "But…", "There was just one problem", "But the phone call never came" | all [V] |
| **Escalation** | every 8-12 s | "But the story got much weirder", "But that was only the beginning", "But it gets even crazier", "And there was one detail that made it even crazier" | 2-4 per Short [V] |
| **Explainers** | anywhere | "You see,", "The thing is,", "What I mean is that", "As it turns out," and first-person asides ("Honestly, I think…", "that was the first match I witnessed in person") | most [V] |
| **Climax** | about 50-60 s | "But then came the moment nobody saw coming" | most [V] |
| **Payoff** | last 5-10 s | "Here's the wild part / here's the twist", then a hard number or irony: "97.5% collapse", "$30 million", "fired hours after the win", "his mum cried" | all [V] |
| **Optional teaser** | after the payoff | "And my next video is about [topic]. Follow if you want the algorithm to show it to you." | 2 of the top 6 Goal Shorts (De Gea, Lampard) and most TikTok copies [V] |

- **One story per Short.** The title sounds like a list; the Short tells one story, sometimes 2-3 escalating ones. [V]
- **Length and pace:**
  - Runtime: median 70-76 s on YouTube (58-167 s). [V]
  - Words: about 200-330 per Short; 228-282 words a minute (n=7). [V]
  - The voice starts at 0.00 s and runs to the last frame. [V]
- **No question ending, ever.** [V]

## 4. Pictures and editing
- **Shots:** a new picture every 1.1-2.1 s (average shot 1.09-2.06 s, n=7). [V]
  - The first shot holds about 2.5 s under the title caption: 0-1 cuts in the first 3 s. [V]
- **Literal matching:**
  - The named person is on screen when named: press photos, interview stills, game footage. [V]
  - Abstract phrases get a literal picture: a phone ringing, a laughing meme face, a game-settings screen, a soccer ball for "amateurs". [V]
- **Framing:** most shots are cropped full-frame. Wide clips sit over a blurred copy of themselves, or play on a TV inside a branded room (an "@AHMEDSGOAL" backdrop). [V]
- **Ending:** the last shot shows the payoff (a crest graphic with the score) or the next video's subject when they tease it. [V]
- **Captions:** measured on their frames. [V]
  - TikTok-style sans (we use **TikTok Sans SemiBold**, size 80 at 1080x1920 = 514 px for "did not slow down").
  - White with a thin black outline, no box.
  - Centred at **y = 960** (dead centre).
  - **2-3 words a card**, sentence case as spoken, punctuation kept.
  - The first card shows the full title on 1-2 lines with 1-2 CAPS words and sometimes an emoji.
- **Audio:**
  - Mono-centred narration. [V]
  - A continuous quiet bed under it: the noise floor never drops below about -45 dB, against digital silence in our own files. [V]
  - YouTube's captions mark "[music]" in some. [V]
  - So: **a quiet music bed about 20 dB under the voice.** [V]
- **Rough edges they accept:** borrowed clips with other creators' captions burned in, watermarks, soft 480p archive. [V] We hold a higher bar on burned-in text; the rest is fine.

## 5. What we copy and what we don't
**Copy:**
- the business model (series titles, cadence, cross-posting);
- the script shape and beat timings;
- 60-80 s runtime and 230-280 words a minute;
- picture density and literal matching;
- the caption look;
- the quiet bed;
- the next-video teaser, as optional.

**Don't copy:**
- their wording, signature phrases or specific stories (we pick our own and check them against their title list);
- their branded room;
- borrowed clips with other creators' captions on them.

**Keep our own standards:**
- every number sourced in `notes.md`;
- the QA gate;
- copy-check before building.
