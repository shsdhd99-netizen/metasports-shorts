Your fingerprints have been the same since before you were born. They form in the womb, around the fourth month, and they stay with you for life. Not even identical twins share them. Look closely, and the ridges split and stop in tiny places. Experts call these details minutiae. When you touch a glass, the moisture and oil on your fingers leave a copy of those ridges behind. Investigators brush it with fine powder, and the hidden print appears. Then a computer searches a database of stored prints and suggests possible matches. But for crime scene prints, the computer usually just suggests. A trained examiner makes the final call. So next time you grab a door handle, remember: you just signed your name.
