How a computer made a video of you.

No camera crew and no editing software on your side. Three separate jobs were done by three separate tools, and we put them together and checked the result.

1 · THE VOICE

It learned your voice

You sent a voice memo of about a minute. A voice program listened and learned how you sound, a bit like a very good impressionist. Then it read our script out loud in that voice.

2 · THE FACE

It animated your photo

You sent one photo. A second program moves the face in that photo (lips, cheeks, a little head motion) so the mouth matches the words. A third step fine-tunes the lips so they line up with each sound.

3 · THE WRITING

We wrote and checked it

We wrote the script, checked every fact against reliable sources, picked the pictures, added captions and music, and made sure the length was right. You did none of that.

Is it really "you"? It is your photo, a computer copy of your voice, and words you can approve. You never recorded those lines. The most important step

Send us better photos and a better recording.

The draft above came from one photo and a short phone memo, so it is the worst version you will ever see. Three simple changes make the biggest difference, and they take about 10 minutes.

Photos (3 to 5)

  • Face the camera, shoulders square, phone at eye height
  • Good light on your face (a window in front of you is perfect), no light behind you
  • Plain, uncluttered background
  • Mouth closed, relaxed in at least two; one natural smile is fine
  • No hats, sunglasses or hair across the eyes

Your voice (2 to 5 minutes)

  • A quiet room with soft things around (a closet or a car is great), no fan or music
  • Phone about a hand-width from your mouth, not on speaker
  • Read the three passages below in your normal teaching voice: warm, not rushed
  • If you make a mistake, pause, then re-read the sentence; no need to start over

Optional: a short video of you (30 to 90 seconds)

Talk to the camera the way you would in class: explain anything you like. This lets us build a much more natural face, with real head and hand movement.

Read these three passages out loud

1. Explain it. "Here's something you do a hundred times a day without noticing. Every time you touch something, you leave your signature behind. Your fingerprints formed before you were born, and they will never change. Not even identical twins share them. Each one is a pattern of ridges that split and stop, and those tiny details are what make it yours."

2. Talk to the class. "Alright, everybody, eyes up here. Today we are going to try something a little different. I want you to think about what you already know, and then I want you to prove me wrong. Ready? Let's go."

3. A few numbers and names. "In nineteen ninety-eight, three hundred and forty-two students joined us from twelve different schools. The lab is open Monday through Thursday, from seven fifteen until four thirty. Questions go to Mister Claude, in room two oh four."
