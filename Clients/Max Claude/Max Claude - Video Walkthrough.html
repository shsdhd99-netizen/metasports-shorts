A 45-second forensic science lesson

This is the kind of video we'd make first. You appear on screen as the teacher; pictures of the evidence fill the screen as you talk.

0:00 Your fingerprints have been the same since before you were born.

0:04 They form in the womb, around the fourth month, and they never change. Not even identical twins share them.

0:12 Every print has ridges that split, stop and loop. Experts call these details minutiae.

0:19 When you touch a glass, sweat and oil leave a copy of those ridges behind. Investigators dust it with fine powder, and the hidden print appears.

0:29 Then a computer compares it against millions of prints in seconds. But the computer only suggests a match. A trained examiner makes the final call.

0:40 So next time you grab a door handle, remember: you just signed your name.

How to get the best quality

Voice
  • A quiet room with soft things around (a closet with clothes works)
  • A USB or lapel microphone, 15–20 cm from your mouth
  • Read naturally, the way you teach; vary your energy
  • 30 minutes minimum; 1–2 hours gives the best clone
Face
  • Phone or camera at eye level, 4K if possible, landscape and steady
  • Light facing you (a window or a ring light), plain background
  • Talk to the lens for 2–5 minutes, small natural hand moves
  • Pause and close your lips between sentences
Not sure what to say? Read this out loud

"Hi, I'm Max. Today I want to explain something most people get wrong. It sounds simple, but the details matter. First, let's look at where it starts. Then I'll show you the part that surprised me most. By the end, you'll see it everywhere."

Read it a few times in different moods (calm, excited, serious), then read any article or book aloud for the rest of the time.
